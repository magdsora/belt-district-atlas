How to read it

Cases are suspected meningitis cases reported to WHO's meningitis dashboard, for one week or the whole year, from 2021. Districts with no cases are left blank; hatching means no report.

Thresholds are the dashboard's alerts and epidemics: epidemic at 10 or more cases per 100,000 people in a week, alert at 3 to 10 per 100,000 with at least 2 cases. Over a whole year, the colour counts the weeks spent over a threshold.

Season shows how bunched each district's cases are in the calendar, in an average year of the years you choose (full years only): each week is averaged over the years that reported it. Strength runs from 0, cases spread evenly through the year, to 1, all of them in the same week. A clear season is 0.5 or more, some season 0.25 to 0.5, and little or no season under 0.25. Districts with fewer than 20 cases in the period get no class. The chart splits each week's cases into those that came in weeks the district was over the threshold and the rest.

Epidemics 2003–22 shows Molly Cliff's published list of districts in epidemic, year by year, coloured by her count for the year, which is the number of months that hold an epidemic week. Her papers call these outbreaks; an epidemic week is the same thing as in the other tabs, 10 or more cases per 100,000 people in a week, and alerts are not in her list. Her list uses administrative units. Where a unit holds several health districts it is drawn whole, with a dashed outline, because the list does not say which district had the epidemic.

Bulletins 2010–20 shows the districts WHO's weekly bulletins named in alert or in epidemic, for one week or a whole year. The bulletins name only the districts over a threshold. Until 2015 an alert was called at 5 cases per 100,000, and from 2016 at 3, so alerts before 2016 are striped and can't be compared with later years. Epidemics, at 10 per 100,000, compare across all years.

Boundary history shows the district and region outlines for each year from 2015, for all 26 countries of the meningitis belt; the other views cover the nine study countries. Outlines that changed from the year before flash orange.

Selecting: click a district or region to keep its details on the right. Cmd-click (Ctrl-click on Windows) to select several and see their totals. Esc clears the selection.

Where the sources disagree

One ID per district. The dashboard, the bulletins, Cliff's list and WHO's map share no district codes. Each name was linked once to a permanent district ID, and everything on this page is joined through those IDs. Names that did not match exactly were checked one by one.

Chad lists far more districts in the dashboard (199 in 2026) than any map has outlines (126), so the views with cases show it by its 23 provinces, each adding up all its districts. The other views draw its districts: WHO's outlines until 2016 and the Chad Ministry of Health's own map from 2017, because WHO's copy puts about a dozen names on the wrong outline.

Outlines in the wrong place. Where WHO's outline misses the district's own town and another outline holds it, the other one is used: 15 districts in Cameroon before 2019, Agadez in Niger from 2017, Obuasi in Ghana before 2018 and Akébou in Togo. Ghana's Kwahu Afram Plains North and South carry each other's names on WHO's current map; here each name sits on the outline that holds its own town. The district's details say when an outline is not WHO's own for that year.

New districts. A district newer than the map is drawn on the outline of the district it was cut from, with their cases added together: Bamako's sub-districts, Cotonou's six districts and eleven Cameroon districts before 2025, and Guan in Ghana. Five Cameroon districts whose parent is not known, and most of Chad's new ones, have no outline. They are counted in their region and listed, never drawn on a guessed shape.

Boundary dates. WHO's map dates a change to when the map was updated, often years late. Its records start in 2000. The first change it records is dated 2011 (Sudan and South Sudan), and 2016 in the nine study countries, where Ghana's 2012 districts appear only from 2018. After 2020 it showed no change until its update of 14 October 2026, which adds outlines dated 2025 in Cameroon, Benin, Kenya and Uganda, and 2021 in Ethiopia and Senegal. They are used here from WHO's dates. WHO now divides Benin's Abomey-Calavi and Porto-Novo into three each, but the dashboard still reports each as one district, so the views with cases keep them whole.
